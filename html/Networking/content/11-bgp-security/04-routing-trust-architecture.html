Routing Trust Architecture and Design Principles

Before picking a feature, write down what a route is allowed to mean. This chapter is that list. Later chapters attach RPKI, IRR, filters, and limits to it.

Learning goals

  • Separate checks you can do with cryptography from checks that stay contractual
  • Place each control in a layer so one failure does not become acceptance
  • Say what Valid, Invalid, and NotFound authorise you to do
  • Give a customer session fewer powers than a transit session

The Routing Trust Problem: What Can We Verify and What Must We Trust?

You can verify, with a relying party and a trust anchor you chose:

  • That a resource certificate chains to an RIR and covers a prefix and an AS number
  • That a ROA under that certificate authorises an origin and a maximum length
  • That an ASPA under that certificate names provider ASes, where the object exists and your router understands it

You cannot verify from the protocol alone:

  • That the neighbour on TCP 179 is the organisation named in the contract. You configured that address
  • That the neighbour’s export policy matches the peering agreement
  • That a path which is valley-free on paper was the path the packets will take
  • That an IRR object was created by the holder, unless the registry’s authentication happens to be sound
  • That a NotFound prefix is safe. Absence of a ROA is not a signature

Design reviews should mark every acceptance rule as either a check or a trust. “Accept the full table from this transit provider” is a trust. It is a reasonable one. It is not a validation result.

Defense in Depth for Internet Routing

Depth means independent layers, not three copies of the same filter.

Layer Fails closed when Still open when
Session scope The peer address, TTL, or TCP-AO does not match The legitimate peer is compromised or misconfigured
Relationship filter The prefix or AS path is outside the cone you listed The list is wrong, or transit is intentionally broad
Origin validation The route is RPKI Invalid and policy drops Invalid The route is NotFound, or Valid and leaked
Path relationship OTC or ASPA says the valley is illegal, and policy drops that The objects do not exist yet, or the peer stripped the signal
Capacity limits The prefix count exceeds the contract The attack fits inside the count
Detection An anomaly is paged and a human acts Nobody is watching, or the page is normal churn

A design that only turns on ROV has one layer. A design that only builds IRR filters has a different single layer, with worse data quality. Production edges run both, plus max-prefix, plus a human loop for the prefixes those layers cannot see.

The Validity Spectrum: From Open to Cryptographically Verified

Treat acceptance as a spectrum you can move along per neighbour.

Posture Behaviour When it is honest
Open Accept the table the neighbour sends Never, on a customer or a peer
Registered Accept what IRR objects say, generated into a filter Customers who will not publish ROAs, with a human spot-check
Monitored Tag ROV state, do not drop The first weeks after you connect a validator
Enforced origin Drop Invalid, keep NotFound, prefer Valid if you have a reason Transit and peer sessions, once you have measured the Invalids
Enforced relationship Also drop ASPA-unknown or ASPA-invalid where coverage and code allow After you have watched the unknowns. Coverage in 2026 does not support dropping unknown

Moving to the right without measuring creates outages that you caused. Chapter 7 is the migration. The spectrum is per session. Your own customer can be on enforced origin while a large peer is still on monitored, if that peer’s customers have a trail of broken ROAs you are still calling about.

Least Privilege for Prefix Announcements

A neighbour receives the minimum authority that matches the contract.

  • A single-homed customer may announce a named prefix list, from their own AS, up to an agreed length, with a max-prefix a little above the list. They may not set your local preference except through communities you defined. They may not re-advertise a path that already contains a transit AS.
  • A peer may announce their cone. You build the cone from their AS-set plus ROAs, you refresh it, and you cap the count.
  • You announce to a transit provider only your cone. Export filters are least privilege in the other direction. A missing export filter is how you become the leaker in someone else’s incident report.

Least privilege also applies inside the AS. Route reflectors do not get a more generous view of external routes than the edge policy already allowed. A looking-glass router that accepts a full table for analysis does not re-advertise it.

Trust Boundaries Across Organizational Perimeters

Draw the boundary at the eBGP session, not at the AS number. Subsidiaries, DDoS scrubbers, cloud private interconnects, and IXP route servers are separate organisations even when the contract feels internal. Each gets its own import policy.

Inside the AS, iBGP is a trusted domain only if every speaker is yours and the management plane is yours. A customer-managed router that you allowed into iBGP is an external party with internal attribute rights. Prefer eBGP with a private AS over that shortcut.

RPKI trust anchors are a different perimeter. You trust the five RIRs for the address space they allocate. A validator you did not install, operated by a stranger, is a new trust anchor wearing an RTR port. Public caches are fine for experiments. Production routers take VRPs from caches you run. Chapter 6 is that split.

Balancing Security, Availability, and Operational Complexity

Routing security that drops too much becomes the outage. Three balances show up in every design in chapter 15.

NotFound stays reachable. Dropping it blackholes a large minority of the table. Around late October 2026, public measurements still put roughly a quarter of announced prefixes in the unknown RPKI state, with a clear majority Valid and under one percent Invalid. Those shares move. The policy implication does not: Invalid is the state you can drop; NotFound is the state you watch.

Fail open on the cache, and notice. If the validator disappears and the router forgets its VRPs, every prefix becomes NotFound and your “drop Invalid” rule stops firing. That preserves reachability and removes the control. Alert on cache state and on a collapsing VRP count. The ROV lab’s cache-disconnect drill is the same failure in miniature.

Complexity budget. A filter you cannot regenerate will drift. A policy only one engineer understands will be bypassed during an incident. Chapters 17 and 20 exist so the strictest rule you are willing to generate automatically wins over a stricter rule that lives in a terminal scrollback.

Summary

  • Cryptography checks resource holder statements. Contracts check neighbours
  • Stack session scope, relationship filters, ROV, path checks, limits, and detection
  • Move along the validity spectrum per neighbour, after measuring
  • A customer session is a small privilege. A transit session is a conscious trust
  • Availability wins when the alternative is dropping NotFound or failing closed on a cache outage
