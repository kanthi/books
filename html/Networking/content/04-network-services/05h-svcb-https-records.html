SVCB and HTTPS Records: Service Binding in the DNS (RFC 9460 and 9461)

For decades, connecting to a web origin required a two-step guessing game. You asked the DNS for A and AAAA records, got back IP addresses with zero protocol metadata, and then probed TCP port 443—only discovering later, via HTTP Alt-Svc headers or failed handshakes, that the server preferred HTTP/3 over QUIC, lived on a different port, or required an Encrypted Client Hello (ECH) configuration. Worse, if you wanted to alias a zone apex like example.com to a CDN hostname, DNS rules forbade a CNAME at the apex alongside SOA and NS records, forcing operators into proprietary ANAME or ALIAS flattening hacks.

RFC 9460 solves both problems with two extensible resource record types: SVCB (Service Binding) and its HTTP-specific sibling HTTPS. Instead of returning bare IP addresses, an HTTPS record binds a service name to a priority, a target hostname, transport protocols (alpn), port overrides, IP address hints, and TLS extensions such as ech—all in a single DNS response. When you only need to alias a domain—including at the zone apex—SVCB and HTTPS provide a standardized AliasMode that coexists legally with SOA and NS.

Other chapters in this book examine the consumers of these records: Happy Eyeballs v3 racing endpoints learned from HTTPS/SVCB, Encrypted Client Hello publishing its ECHConfigList inside HTTPS, and Discovery of Designated Resolvers (DDR, RFC 9462) using SVCB at _dns.resolver.arpa with DNS-specific keys from RFC 9461. This chapter focuses on the record itself: its two modes, its wire encoding, its parameter ordering rules, and how to decode and validate real answers.

Learning goals

After working through this chapter, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish SVCB from HTTPS and explain when a client queries each RR type.
  • Contrast AliasMode (priority 0) and ServiceMode (priority != 0), including how HTTPS at a zone apex avoids the CNAME-at-apex restriction.
  • Decode and validate the SVCB/HTTPS wire format: SvcPriority, uncompressed TargetName, and strictly increasing (key, length, value) SvcParam tuples.
  • Interpret well-known SvcParamKeys (mandatory, alpn, no-default-alpn, port, ipv4hint, ech, ipv6hint) and enforce RFC 9460’s mandatory and unknown-key rules.
  • Explain how RFC 9461 extends SVCB for DNS transport discovery without conflating hints with authoritative address records.
  • Inspect live HTTPS responses over DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH) and catch malformed encodings matching RFC 9460 Appendix D failure cases.

SVCB vs. HTTPS and the Two Modes

SVCB is a general-purpose service binding record. For non-HTTP protocols, clients query SVCB at an underscore-prefixed service name (or a designated name such as _dns.resolver.arpa for DDR). HTTPS is a SVCB-compatible RR type dedicated to the https URI scheme (default port 443) and queried directly at the origin hostname—or at _<port>._https.<host> when an https URL specifies a non-default port. Both SVCB and HTTPS share the exact same wire format and parameter registry; only the RR type code and default scheme semantics differ.

Every SVCB or HTTPS record operates in one of two mutually exclusive modes, determined solely by its 16-bit SvcPriority field:

  SvcPriority == 0  ──▶  AliasMode
                         - TargetName is the canonical alias
                         - MUST NOT carry any SvcParams
                         - Client follows TargetName (HTTPS/SVCB + A/AAAA)

  SvcPriority != 0  ──▶  ServiceMode
                         - Lower number = higher preference (tried first)
                         - TargetName "." means "this exact owner name"
                         - May carry SvcParams in strictly increasing key order

AliasMode and the Zone Apex

In classic DNS, you cannot place a CNAME at the zone apex (for example, example.com) because CNAME cannot coexist with the SOA and NS records required at every zone cut. Operators had to either host an HTTP redirector at the apex or rely on authoritative servers synthesizing A/AAAA records on the fly.

An HTTPS record in AliasMode (priority = 0) is not a CNAME. It is a normal RRset that sits cleanly alongside SOA, NS, and MX at the apex:

example.com.  300  IN  HTTPS  0  cdn.example.net.

When a client sees priority = 0, it treats cdn.example.net. as an alias for the https service, follows the chain (querying HTTPS and A/AAAA at cdn.example.net.), and never expects SvcParams on the AliasMode record itself. If an AliasMode record contains any SvcParams, the record is malformed per RFC 9460 and must be ignored.

ServiceMode and Priority Ordering

Any non-zero SvcPriority (1 through 65535) puts the record in ServiceMode. Lower numbers are preferred first, and a TargetName of . (the DNS root label, 0x00 on the wire) is a shorthand meaning “use the owner name of this record as the target.”

If a domain publishes multiple ServiceMode records—say, priority 1 pointing to a primary CDN endpoint and priority 2 pointing to a fallback origin—clients attempt them in ascending priority order, applying Happy Eyeballs-style endpoint racing as covered in the Happy Eyeballs v3 chapter.

Wire Format and SvcParamKey Ordering

In DNS zone files (presentation format), SVCB and HTTPS records are human-readable and may list SvcParams in any order:

www.example.com. 300 IN HTTPS 1 . alpn="h2,h3" ipv4hint=192.0.2.1

On the wire, however, the RDATA layout is rigid:

+-------------------------------+
|     SvcPriority (2 octets)    |  uint16, network byte order
+-------------------------------+
|                               |
/     TargetName (variable)     /  DNS wire name, UNCOMPRESSED
|                               |
+-------------------------------+
|    SvcParamKey (2 octets)     |  uint16, strictly increasing
+-------------------------------+
|  SvcParamValueLen (2 octets)  |  uint16, byte length of value
+-------------------------------+
|                               |
/    SvcParamValue (variable)   /  SvcParamValueLen octets
|                               |
+-------------------------------+
|     ... more SvcParams ...    |
+-------------------------------+

Three wire rules trip up custom encoders and parsers most often:

  1. Uncompressed TargetName: Unlike MX or CNAME in standard DNS messages, TargetName in SVCB/HTTPS RDATA must never use DNS name compression pointers (0xC0xx). A root target . is always a single 0x00 octet.
  2. Strictly increasing SvcParamKeys: Presentation format lets you write ipv4hint before alpn, but the encoder must sort SvcParams by numeric key ID before serializing to the wire. Duplicate keys and out-of-order keys are illegal.
  3. Opaque extensibility: Every SvcParam carries an explicit 2-octet byte length, so a parser that does not recognize key N can skip SvcParamValueLen bytes cleanly—unless key N is listed in mandatory.

Well-Known SvcParamKeys

RFC 9460 defines the core SvcParamKey registry. Clients skip unknown keys silently unless those keys appear in mandatory (key 0).

Key ID Name Wire Value Format Meaning & Constraints
0 mandatory Sequence of 2-octet key IDs (strictly increasing) Keys the client must understand to use this record. Must not include 0 itself; every listed key must be present in the record.
1 alpn Sequence of (1-octet len \|\| protocol ID) Supported ALPNs in preference order (e.g., h3, h2). Commas and backslashes are escaped in presentation format.
2 no-default-alpn Empty (0 octets) Client must not fall back to the scheme’s default ALPN (http/1.1 for https). Non-empty value is illegal.
3 port Exactly 2 octets (uint16) Alternative TCP/UDP port instead of the scheme default (443). Empty or != 2 octets is illegal.
4 ipv4hint Concatenated 4-octet IPv4 addresses Connection-racing hints only; not a substitute for authoritative A records.
5 ech ECHConfigList bytes Encrypted Client Hello public configuration (covered in the ECH chapter).
6 ipv6hint Concatenated 16-octet IPv6 addresses Connection-racing hints only; not a substitute for authoritative AAAA records.

Mandatory Keys and Safe Extensibility

The mandatory parameter lets a server require support for a newer extension before a client uses a ServiceMode record. For example, if a record lists mandatory=alpn,ech, a legacy client that understands alpn (1) but does not understand ech (5) must reject that ServiceMode record and fall back to the next lower-priority record (or to standard A/AAAA if no usable ServiceMode record remains).

To prevent circular or impossible requirements, RFC 9460 enforces two strict invariants on mandatory: - mandatory (0) must never list mandatory (0) itself. - Every key ID listed in mandatory must actually be present in the same record’s SvcParams.

Hints Are Not Address Records

ipv4hint and ipv6hint exist to eliminate round-trip stalls. A client that receives an HTTPS answer containing hints can immediately initiate TCP/QUIC connection attempts to those addresses while waiting for—or before receiving—A and AAAA responses. However, RFC 9460 is explicit: hints are not a substitute for authoritative A/AAAA records. Once authoritative A/AAAA records arrive, the client uses them as the source of truth (and honors their TTLs and DNSSEC validation).

DNS-Specific Keys (RFC 9461)

RFC 9461 adapts SVCB for DNS transport discovery, pairing with Discovery of Designated Resolvers (DDR, RFC 9462). Instead of web ALPNs like h2 or h3, DNS SVCB records carry DNS-specific parameters such as alpn=dot (DNS-over-TLS), alpn=h2 with dohpath (a URI template for DNS-over-HTTPS, e.g., /dns-query{?dns}), and port overrides. The encrypted-DNS chapter covers how resolvers and clients discover and authenticate these records at _dns.resolver.arpa.

The lab

This lab verifies RFC 9460 wire encoding and decoding against all authoritative test vectors in RFC 9460 Appendix D—both the valid records (including sorted key canonicalization in Figure 9 and escaped alpn strings in Figure 10) and every malformed encoding in Appendix D.3—then queries four live domains over DoH (cloudflare-dns.com) to inspect real-world HTTPS deployments.

The lab program is svcb-lab.py. It checks every RFC 9460 Appendix D success vector and every D.3 failure case, then issues live HTTPS queries over DoH to cloudflare-dns.com.

svcb-lab.py:

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""SVCB/HTTPS (RFC 9460) codec + Appendix D vectors + live DoH probes."""
from __future__ import annotations
import base64, socket, ssl, struct, sys, urllib.request

KEYS = {
    0: "mandatory", 1: "alpn", 2: "no-default-alpn", 3: "port",
    4: "ipv4hint", 5: "ech", 6: "ipv6hint", 7: "dohpath",
}
KEY_NUM = {v: k for k, v in KEYS.items()}

def encode_name(name: str) -> bytes:
    if name in (".", ""):
        return b"\x00"
    out = b""
    for label in name.rstrip(".").split("."):
        raw = label.encode("ascii")
        out += bytes([len(raw)]) + raw
    return out + b"\x00"

def decode_name(buf: bytes, off: int = 0) -> tuple[str, int]:
    labels = []
    if buf[off] == 0:
        return ".", off + 1
    while True:
        n = buf[off]
        if n == 0:
            return ".".join(labels) + ".", off + 1
        if n & 0xC0:
            raise ValueError("compression pointer not allowed in SVCB TargetName")
        labels.append(buf[off+1:off+1+n].decode("ascii"))
        off += 1 + n

def encode_alpn(ids: list[str]) -> bytes:
    out = b""
    for i in ids:
        raw = i.encode("ascii")
        out += bytes([len(raw)]) + raw
    return out

def decode_alpn(val: bytes) -> list[str]:
    out, off = [], 0
    while off < len(val):
        n = val[off]; off += 1
        out.append(val[off:off+n].decode("ascii")); off += n
    return out

def encode_svcb(priority: int, target: str, params: dict[int, bytes]) -> bytes:
    if priority == 0 and params:
        raise ValueError("AliasMode (priority 0) must not carry SvcParams")
    body = struct.pack("!H", priority) + encode_name(target)
    for k in sorted(params):
        v = params[k]
        body += struct.pack("!HH", k, len(v)) + v
    return body

def decode_svcb(rdata: bytes) -> tuple[int, str, dict[int, bytes]]:
    if len(rdata) < 3:
        raise ValueError("truncated")
    pri = struct.unpack_from("!H", rdata, 0)[0]
    target, off = decode_name(rdata, 2)
    params = {}
    last = -1
    while off < len(rdata):
        if off + 4 > len(rdata):
            raise ValueError("truncated param")
        k, ln = struct.unpack_from("!HH", rdata, off)
        off += 4
        if k <= last:
            raise ValueError(f"SvcParamKeys not strictly increasing: {k} after {last}")
        last = k
        if off + ln > len(rdata):
            raise ValueError("truncated param value")
        params[k] = rdata[off:off+ln]
        off += ln
    if pri == 0 and params:
        raise ValueError("AliasMode with SvcParams")
    return pri, target, params

def hex_of(b: bytes) -> str:
    return " ".join(f"{x:02x}" for x in b)

def dns_query(name: str, typ: int) -> bytes:
    ident = 0x2111
    header = struct.pack("!HHHHHH", ident, 0x0100, 1, 0, 0, 0)
    q = encode_name(name) + struct.pack("!HH", typ, 1)
    return header + q

def parse_answers(msg: bytes, typ: int) -> list[bytes]:
    # skip header + question, then walk answers (no name compression handling beyond pointers)
    if len(msg) < 12:
        raise ValueError("short DNS message")
    id_, flags, qd, an, ns, ar = struct.unpack_from("!HHHHHH", msg, 0)
    rcode = flags & 0xF
    off = 12
    def skip_name(o):
        while True:
            if o >= len(msg):
                raise ValueError("bad name")
            n = msg[o]
            if n == 0:
                return o + 1
            if n & 0xC0:
                return o + 2
            o += 1 + n
    for _ in range(qd):
        off = skip_name(off) + 4
    rdata = []
    for _ in range(an):
        off = skip_name(off)
        atype, aclass, _ttl, rdlen = struct.unpack_from("!HHIH", msg, off)
        off += 10
        if atype == typ and aclass == 1:
            rdata.append(msg[off:off+rdlen])
        off += rdlen
    return rcode, flags, rdata

def doh_query(name: str, typ: int) -> tuple[int, list[bytes]]:
    q = dns_query(name, typ)
    url = "https://cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query"
    req = urllib.request.Request(
        url, data=q,
        headers={"Content-Type": "application/dns-message", "Accept": "application/dns-message"},
        method="POST",
    )
    ctx = ssl.create_default_context()
    with urllib.request.urlopen(req, context=ctx, timeout=15) as resp:
        msg = resp.read()
    rcode, flags, rdata = parse_answers(msg, typ)
    return rcode, rdata

def present(pri, target, params) -> str:
    bits = [f"priority={pri}", f"target={target}"]
    for k, v in params.items():
        name = KEYS.get(k, f"key{k}")
        if k == 1:
            bits.append(f"alpn={decode_alpn(v)}")
        elif k == 3:
            bits.append(f"port={struct.unpack('!H', v)[0]}")
        elif k == 4:
            addrs = [socket.inet_ntop(socket.AF_INET, v[i:i+4]) for i in range(0, len(v), 4)]
            bits.append(f"ipv4hint={addrs}")
        elif k == 6:
            addrs = [socket.inet_ntop(socket.AF_INET6, v[i:i+16]) for i in range(0, len(v), 16)]
            bits.append(f"ipv6hint={addrs}")
        elif k == 0:
            keys = [KEYS.get(struct.unpack_from('!H', v, i)[0], str(struct.unpack_from('!H', v, i)[0]))
                    for i in range(0, len(v), 2)]
            bits.append(f"mandatory={keys}")
        elif k == 7:
            bits.append(f"dohpath={v.decode('ascii', 'replace')}")
        elif k == 5:
            bits.append(f"ech={len(v)} bytes")
        else:
            bits.append(f"{name}={v!r}")
    return " ".join(bits)

Gotchas

  • The trap: publishing stale or wrong ipv4hint/ipv6hint values. Because Happy Eyeballs v3 clients may race hinted addresses immediately before A/AAAA answers arrive, a stale hint sends initial SYN or QUIC Initial packets to the wrong host. If you automate CDN or load-balancer IP changes, update ipv4hint/ipv6hint in lockstep with A/AAAA—or omit hints entirely (as www.google.com does) and let A/AAAA carry the addresses.
  • The trap: assuming presentation order equals wire order. Zone files and CLI tools often display SvcParams in arbitrary order, or let you write ipv4hint before alpn. On the wire, SvcParamKeys must be strictly increasing. Any custom packet builder or signer that serializes keys in input order produces an invalid RR that strict resolvers and clients drop.
  • The trap: attaching SvcParams to AliasMode. An AliasMode record (priority = 0) is purely a pointer. Adding alpn, port, or hints to a priority-0 record violates RFC 9460 and causes compliant parsers to discard the entire record.
  • The trap: treating hints as a replacement for A/AAAA. Hints are unauthenticated shortcuts when A/AAAA are delayed; they do not replace A/AAAA resolution or DNSSEC validation of address records. Never configure a client or resolver to skip A/AAAA lookups permanently just because hints were present.
  • The trap: middlebox stripping of HTTPS/SVCB. In some enterprise or ISP networks, DNS forwarders or interception proxies strip or block HTTPS queries and responses—often to prevent clients from learning ech keys and hiding the TLS SNI. When troubleshooting missing ECH or unexpected http/1.1 fallbacks, compare what the authoritative nameserver returns against what the local stub resolver receives.
  • The trap: using CNAME at the zone apex instead of HTTPS AliasMode. Placing a CNAME at example.com breaks SOA/NS coexistence and violates DNS standards. Use HTTPS 0 target.example.net. for https origins at the apex, rather than relying on non-standard zone-flattening workarounds where HTTPS AliasMode is supported.

The boring rule

Always sort SvcParamKeys in strictly increasing numeric order on the wire, keep AliasMode completely free of SvcParams, and treat ipv4hint/ipv6hint as racing hints rather than authoritative replacements for A and AAAA.
